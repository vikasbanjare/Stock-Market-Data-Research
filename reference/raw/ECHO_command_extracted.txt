If you woke up after the first three months of 2026, not knowing what happened in the first 
90 days, you would be a happy investor. Especially if you had invested in the Nifty 50. The 
first seven days of April 2026 have returned investors 7.7%. 

The first three months of 2026? A negative 14.5%. The pendulum has swung so far back 
after the markets bled in the first calendar quarter that nearly half of the losses in 2026 
have been wiped out in the first seven trading sessions of April 2026. 

That is what a turn of sentiment brings to investing in the stock market. It’s not for the faint 
of heart, but this moment allows investors to reflect on how 10 days can change the 
context for markets. 

A ceasefire agreement between the US, Israel and Iran sent Brent Crude futures below 
$100 for the second consecutive day on Friday. This was the first time this has happened in 
three weeks. 

This has produced the outsized movement in benchmark indices in India. Is this an over-
reaction? Only time will tell. But one thing is for sure, as things start settling down, there 
will be more moonshot targets for benchmark indices that will come our way.

Banks staged one of the strongest late-week recoveries and helped lift the broader market 
when sentiment improved. ICICI Bank and Axis Bank attracted strong buying in the final 
session, showing that investors were willing to return to large private lenders after the 

 
 
 
 
earlier weakness. The sector staged a strong comeback toward the end of the week, 
supported by the RBI’s decision to keep the repo rate unchanged, which signaled stability 
in interest rates.  

Real estate emerged as one of the week’s surprise outperformers toward the end. Lodha 
Developers and Godrej Properties led that bounce as investors selectively returned to 
beaten-down realty names and treated the sector as a rebound trade after heavy 
correction. At the same time, the sector’s gains came after a period of visible weakness, so 
the move looked more like a relief rally than a clean all-clear. That means realty ended the 
week stronger, but still sensitive to rates and broader market confidence.  

Autos delivered a more balanced week than many expected. On the positive side, Eicher 
Motors gained traction after its move into the electric motorcycle space, while Maruti 
Suzuki and other auto names found support on recovery days as investors looked past the 
worst of the earlier panic. But the sector did not move in one direction all week. Rising fuel 
costs, input-cost concerns, and weak global sentiment kept the broader auto pack volatile, 
and many investors still treated rallies as opportunities to stay selective rather than chase 
aggressively. So, autos showed resilience, but not immunity.  

FMCG gave the market one of its steadier pockets of support. Asian Paints opened sharply 
higher and consumer-facing names found buyers as investors looked for relatively stable 
businesses during a volatile week. The sector also benefited from the usual defensive 
rotation that appears when investors want earnings visibility over excitement. However, the 
strength remained selective rather than broad-based, and some consumer names like 
United Breweries still struggled to inspire strong conviction because investors preferred 
only the most established quality plays. FMCG, therefore, acted more like a shelter than a 
leadership sector.  

IT services was the week’s clear laggard. Even though TCS reported jump in Q4 profit and 
strong revenue growth, investors sold the stock after the results, and that weakness spread 
across the space. Infosys and HCL Technologies also fell as the market worried more 
about global tech spending, AI-related uncertainty, and whether earnings momentum 
could sustain at a time of cautious client budgets. This made IT one of the rare sectors 
where good numbers failed to create confidence, which tells you how fragile the sentiment 
really was among investors and traders.  

 
 
 
In metals, Tata Steel managed a better session earlier in the week and outperformed in a 
weaker market, showing how stock-specific moves mattered more than broad sector 
strength. The takeaway was clear: investors did not buy the whole sector story here. They 
moved selectively, rewarding stock-specific resilience and punishing names where 
sentiment weakened.

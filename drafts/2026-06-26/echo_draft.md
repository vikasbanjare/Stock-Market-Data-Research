If you blinked during this holiday-shortened week, you missed one of the
sharpest mood swings of the year. A ceasefire between the US and Iran —
and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz — took a sledgehammer to the
fear premium across markets: Brent crude collapsed nearly 10%, and
India's own volatility gauge, the India VIX, crumbled from above 27 to
around 13 in just four trading sessions.

That cocktail — cheaper oil, calmer nerves — helped Indian equities
quietly outperform a world in turmoil. While a brutal global tech
selloff dragged the Nasdaq down more than 4% and sent Seoul's KOSPI
crashing through a circuit breaker, the Nifty 50 ended the week
modestly higher.

Foreign investors stayed net buyers of Indian equities for the second
straight week, adding roughly $788 million — a useful vote of confidence
while the rest of the world was busy selling technology stocks.

## Stocks & Sectors in the News

Pharma and healthcare led the market. The Nifty Pharma index gained
about 2.1% and healthcare about 1.9% for the week, with **Cipla** and
**Dr. Reddy's Laboratories** among the top Nifty 100 gainers and
**Aurobindo Pharma**, **Laurus Labs** and **Zydus Lifesciences** hitting
52-week highs. With global cues wobbly, investors treated the sector as
the week's safe harbour with earnings visibility.

Banks and financials delivered another steady week, with the Bank Nifty
up 0.85% and quietly outperforming most large-cap indices. The RBI's
liquidity-boosting measures, including facilitating lending against
foreign-currency deposits, supported sentiment, and **Federal Bank** and
**Aditya Birla Capital** touched 52-week highs. **Cholamandalam
Investment & Finance** was among the week's top gainers as NBFCs
benefited from the improving rate and liquidity backdrop.

Autos were the top-performing sector of the week. **Tata Motors** led
the charge among large-caps, with the crude collapse promising cheaper
input and fuel costs just as companies prepared to report monthly sales
numbers. **Bharat Forge**'s 52-week high underlined the strength
running through the wider auto-components space.

IT services had a bruising week and was the market's clear laggard.
**Infosys**, **TCS** and **Wipro** all slumped to 52-week lows as
Accenture's guidance cut reverberated through the sector, JP Morgan
downgraded **HCL Technologies** and **Wipro**, and a global tech rout —
the Nasdaq fell more than 4% — removed any appetite for the sector.

Metals bore the week's heaviest selling. **Vedanta**, **Hindustan
Zinc**, **Jindal Steel** and **Hindalco** filled four of the five top
loser slots as the US–Iran ceasefire deflated commodities across the
board, from silver to copper. Rising delivery volumes on the way down
suggest investors were genuinely reducing exposure, not just trading
the volatility.

Aviation and realty rounded out the winners. **InterGlobe Aviation**
soared over 8% on the crude collapse, while realty's roughly 1.8% weekly
gain — and **Macrotech/Lodha**-style rate sensitivity across the sector —
reflected the friendlier liquidity backdrop. FMCG also found buyers as
falling input costs sweetened the margin outlook.

## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

Energy prices came crashing back to earth. Brent crude plunged nearly
10% to close just under $72 a barrel as the US–Iran ceasefire and the
reopening of the Strait of Hormuz drained the war premium out of oil —
even an attack on a cargo ship near Oman couldn't reignite the fear.
Natural gas, by contrast, barely moved, ending the week flat as supply
and demand stayed comfortably balanced.

Gold lost its shine the same way it had gained it: through geopolitics.
With the ceasefire holding, safe-haven demand unwound and gold futures
slipped about 3.5% to around $4,079.

Copper fell nearly 4%, caught in the same commodity-wide deflation, as
traders reassessed how much of the metal's recent strength was
risk-hedging rather than real demand.

- Silver was the week's biggest commodity casualty, sinking over 10% as
  the safe-haven unwind hit the more volatile precious metal hardest.

## Global Signals

- US markets had a rough ride: the S&P 500 slid nearly 2% and the
  Nasdaq tumbled over 4% as a semiconductor-led selloff — sparked in
  part by Micron — swept through technology stocks. The Dow, with its
  lighter tech exposure, actually rose 0.6%, telling you exactly where
  the pain was concentrated.
- Asia bore the brunt. Seoul's KOSPI plunged so hard that the exchange
  triggered a circuit breaker, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix
  hammered on AI-valuation worries, while Japan's Nikkei 225 fell over
  6% for the week.
- European markets ended only moderately lower — their smaller tech
  exposure cushioned the blow, though banks and industrials still
  dragged the STOXX 600 down.
- Against that backdrop, India's flat-to-positive week counts as
  striking outperformance, helped by the oil collapse and its own
  supportive central bank.

# Echo

**1st August to 7th August**

Some weeks the market rewards courage. This week it rewarded metal.

While the Reserve Bank of India was busy doing precisely nothing — and
being cheered for it — silver quietly staged one of its biggest weekly
surges in years, gold marched to a seven-week high, and the mining
stocks attached to them lit up the gainers' board. The trigger sat half
a world away: America's private sector added just 44,000 jobs in July,
the weakest showing since January, and markets promptly unwound their
bets on further Federal Reserve rate hikes. Falling yields, a softer
dollar — and everything that glitters caught a bid.

At home, the RBI's unanimous decision to hold rates at 5.25% came
gift-wrapped with an upgraded growth forecast and a trimmed inflation
projection — a central bank telling the market, in effect, that the
economy can walk without crutches. The Nifty pushed through 24,600 on
policy day before Friday's twist: fresh RBI draft lending norms that
knocked the very NBFCs that led last week's rally.

## Stocks & Sectors in the News

Metals owned the week. **Hindustan Zinc** and **Hindalco** surged as
silver posted a double-digit weekly gain and gold climbed to seven-week
highs — the weak US jobs print unwound rate-hike fears globally, and
the RBI's newly minted permission for silver-collateral lending added a
domestic sparkle to the story. **Vedanta** joined the move in the
broader complex. The rally was profit-driven, not panic-driven: this
time the metals rose while oil fell.

Financials and NBFCs lived a week of two halves. Until Thursday the
sector basked in the RBI's growth-friendly hold; on Friday the central
bank's draft lending norms turned the mood, with **Muthoot Finance**,
**Bajaj Finance**, **Bajaj Finserv** and **Shriram Finance** sliding as
investors parsed what tighter gold-loan and lending rules could mean
for margins. Banks took it in stride — **Union Bank** and PSU peers
outperformed, and **State Bank of India**'s results landed on Friday
with the market still digesting them.

Telecom delivered the week's cleanest earnings beat. **Bharti Airtel**
jumped over 3% after posting a 37.3% rise in Q1 net profit to ₹8,167
crore, with wireless gains against **Jio** and continued strength in
Africa — a reminder that the tariff-repair story still has legs.

Automobiles and components kept their earnings-season momentum.
**Samvardhana Motherson**'s double-digit weekly gain led the component
makers, two-wheeler names stayed supported after last month's strong
results from **TVS Motor** and **Bajaj Auto**, and passenger-vehicle
demand commentary remained constructive into the festive build-up.

The PSU energy pack lagged. **Power Grid** slipped after its results
disappointed, **GAIL** followed, and **ONGC**'s numbers early in the
week failed to excite even as crude's slide — Brent fell nearly 9% as
Iran and Oman moved toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz — flattered
the import bill more than the producers.

Pharma took a breather. **Torrent Pharmaceuticals** cooled off its
recent highs, and the sector consolidated after leading the market in
July — profit-taking more than punishment, with the earnings backdrop
still supportive.

## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

Energy kept deflating, and this time diplomacy did the work: Brent slid
toward $82 a barrel as Iran and Oman closed in on a deal to reopen the
Strait of Hormuz, promising freer energy flows from West Asia. Natural
gas drifted about 4% lower alongside, with supply comfortable and no
weather scare in sight.

Gold had its best week in months, climbing nearly 8% to a seven-week
high — the weak US jobs report took Federal Reserve rate hikes off the
table in traders' minds, and a non-yielding asset loves nothing more
than falling yields.

Silver stole the show outright, surging around 11% as the same
rate-relief trade met tight physical supply — and, at home, the RBI's
new nod to silver as loan collateral gave the white metal an extra
Indian audience.

Copper rose about 2.5%, quietly extending its grid-and-EV demand story
while the spotlight was elsewhere.

## Global Signals

The week's global mood was set by one number: 44,000 — the weakest US
private-sector jobs reading since January. Markets read it as the end
of the Federal Reserve's rate-hike talk, yields slipped, and beaten-up
technology stocks found their feet, with the Nasdaq snapping a six-day
losing streak on Thursday even as worries about Chinese chip
competition and AI spending lingered.

Asia had a heavier week. Japan's markets fell as a powerful earthquake
struck Kumamoto in the Kyushu region — the strongest since the Noto
quake of 2024 — leaving investors weighing the human and economic toll.
Chinese markets stayed subdued on familiar growth concerns.

Europe leaned on the oil story: cheaper energy and progress toward
reopening the Strait of Hormuz supported sentiment across the region's
importers, leaving European equities comparatively steady through the
week.

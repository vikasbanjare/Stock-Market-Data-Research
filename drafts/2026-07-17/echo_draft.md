## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

Brent crude was the week's runaway story, surging nearly 16% to close
at $88.10 — and briefly topping $90 — as renewed tensions between the
US and Iran revived fears of supply disruptions from West Asia. For an
oil importer like India, that translates directly into costlier fuel,
freight and a fatter import bill. Natural gas went the other way,
easing about 1% as comfortable US storage levels kept near-term supply
worries at bay.

Gold slipped around 2% as a stronger dollar and elevated bond yields
dulled the metal's appeal — an unusual week in which the fear trade
flowed into oil rather than bullion. Silver fell harder, down over 6%.

Copper ended marginally lower. Tight supply, smelter maintenance and
demand from power grids, EVs and data centres supported prices, but
worries about slowing consumption in China — the world's largest copper
user — capped any strength.

## Global Signals

Asian markets had a difficult week as the selloff in technology and
semiconductor stocks spread across the region. Japan's Nikkei 225,
Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's key indices all came under pressure,
with Taiwan seeing particularly sharp selling in chip names. Worries
that AI-linked stocks had run up too fast triggered profit booking,
while Middle East tensions and costlier crude kept risk appetite in
check.

European markets moved cautiously, balancing encouraging corporate
earnings against geopolitical and inflation worries. Technology
weakened with the global chip selloff, though London's FTSE 100 held
up better thanks to its lighter tech exposure. The region closed the
week with a mixed-to-negative tone.

Wall Street ended the week in the red across all three major indices.
The Nasdaq fell hardest as investors booked profits in AI-related
names, while strong corporate earnings elsewhere cushioned the S&P 500
and the Dow. Rising bond yields — fed partly by oil's inflationary
impulse — kept the mood cautious.

# Echo

**8th August to 14th August**

A week ago, the Reserve Bank handed this market a gift: lower inflation
forecasts and a friendly hold. This week, the economy returned the
receipt.

July's retail inflation printed at 4.45% — a 19-month high, powered by
food prices — and in a single Wednesday morning the conversation
flipped from "how long can rates stay easy" to "could the next move be
up?" Add a fresh flare in West Asia — deadly attacks on vessels in the
Red Sea and Gulf of Oman that walked crude back toward $89 — and the
market spent its pre-Independence Day week doing what markets do when
two comfortable assumptions break at once: stepping carefully
backwards.

Foreign investors told the story in miniature: buyers on Monday, sellers
every session after the CPI landed. Domestic institutions, as ever,
stood on the other side of the trade — nearly ₹9,000 crore of buying,
their heaviest lifting coming precisely on the inflation-shock days.

## Stocks & Sectors in the News

Financials carried the week's biggest question mark. The CPI shock
revived rate-hike talk seven days after the RBI's dovish hold, and
banks wore the whiplash — **ICICI Bank** lagged through the back half
of the week while the sector digested what a 4.45% print does to the
easy-policy story. NBFCs, still bruised by last week's draft lending
norms, found no fresh reason to rally.

Autos split on results. **Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles**' 6%
results-day drop — margins squeezed by raw material costs and a subdued
JLR — dominated the tape, while the two-wheeler and components names
that led July's earnings season consolidated their gains. The sector's
festive-season demand story remains intact; its margin story just got
harder.

Energy and PSU names found a bid as crude re-tightened. **NTPC** and
**BEL** were among Thursday's leaders, defence names stayed firm, and
the oil complex watched the Red Sea attacks push Brent back toward $89
— a reversal of the previous week's Hormuz-driven relief.

Metals cooled after their monster week. **Hindalco** slipped among the
Friday laggards as the complex consolidated its silver-and-gold-driven
surge; the longer-term grid-and-EV demand thesis for the sector is
unchanged, but the fast money moved on.

Healthcare traded results-heavy. **Apollo Hospitals** and **Max
Healthcare** saw sharp two-way moves around earnings, with Max among
Friday's notable decliners — a reminder that in this market, even
defensive sectors are being priced quarter by quarter.

IT stayed quiet — for once neither the hero nor the villain, with the
sector marking time while the rate and currency stories evolved around
it.

## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

## FIIs vs DIIs

nearly ₹2,000 crore bought on Monday, then steady selling from the
moment Wednesday's inflation print landed, leaving them barely ₹720
crore positive for the four sessions to Thursday. The three-week buying
streak survives, but only just, and the CPI surprise is plainly the
reason.

Domestic institutions did their heaviest work on exactly the days
foreign money left: over ₹5,800 crore of buying on CPI day itself and
₹4,350 crore more on Thursday, closing the four sessions nearly ₹9,000
crore net positive — the year's familiar pattern, holding firm under
pressure.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

## Global Signals

The global mood steadied even as India's wobbled. US markets firmed
through the week as rate expectations found a floor, and Friday's
improving global cues stood in contrast to Dalal Street's caution.

Asia traded mixed: Japan continued rebuilding sentiment after the
Kumamoto earthquake, while Chinese markets stayed range-bound on the
same growth questions that have shadowed them all year.

The energy re-flare was the week's shared global anxiety — vessel
attacks in the Red Sea and Gulf of Oman put shipping costs and
insurance premiums back on every strategist's worksheet, with Brent's
climb toward $89 the visible symptom.

The Reserve Bank of India finally showed its hand, and the market barely
blinked. The central bank kept the repo rate unchanged at 5.25% with a
neutral stance — exactly what most of the street expected — though it
tempered the cheer by trimming its FY27 growth forecast to 6.6% and
nudging its inflation projection up to 5.1%.

What the policy couldn't soothe, global nerves rattled. Tensions around
the Strait of Hormuz kept investors on edge early in the week, and a
sharp Friday selloff — which brokerages pinned partly on MSCI's index
rebalancing, with an estimated ₹8,000 crore of outflows — dragged the
benchmarks to a lower weekly close.

Foreign investors sold Indian equities in all five sessions of the week.
Domestic institutions bought just as relentlessly, which is a big part of
why the damage stayed contained to under a percent.

## Stocks & Sectors in the News

Banks and financials were the week's anchor. The RBI's unanimous decision
to hold the repo rate at 5.25% with a neutral stance gave lenders a
stable rate backdrop, and PSU banks gained through the week while **Axis
Bank** and **Bajaj Finance** attracted buying on policy day. The
sector's resilience stood out all the more because **HDFC Bank** was
among the index drags on Friday, showing the strength was selective
rather than universal.

IT services had a week to forget. **Wipro** was among the worst
large-cap performers, and the pressure intensified after a global
technology selloff on Friday — a semiconductor-led slide wiped an
estimated $1 trillion off global markets as a hot US jobs report pushed
Treasury yields higher and priced out hopes of Federal Reserve rate cuts
this year. For Indian IT, dearer-for-longer US rates and jittery tech
spending are an unhelpful combination.

FMCG told a split-screen story. **Hindustan Unilever** was one of the
week's best large-cap gainers, yet the FMCG index was the worst
performing sector of the week — evidence that investors were paying up
for a few quality defensives while selling the rest of the consumer
pack. [FILL IN: verify which FMCG names dragged the index]

Metals bore the brunt of the global risk-off mood. **Hindalco** fell the
most among large-caps as the Friday rout in global markets and a
stronger dollar weighed on the complex. With US yields surging after the
jobs report, rate-sensitive commodity demand expectations took a knock.

Realty ended the week lower overall, though the sector closed higher on
policy day itself as the RBI's hold kept borrowing-cost fears at bay.
The week's pattern suggested investors are happy to trade realty
tactically around rate events but aren't yet committing to the sector
while the market chops sideways.

Energy and oil & gas names lagged as **Reliance Industries** weighed on
the indices on Friday. Brent crude sliding to a near two-month low
around $89 a barrel is a mixed blessing here — good news for India's
import bill and OMC margins, less good for upstream realisations — and
the sector traded with that ambiguity all week. Media, for what it's
worth, was the week's surprise champion, with the sector index surging
over 6%.

## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

## Global Signals

US markets ended the week with a thud. A blowout May jobs report —
roughly twice what economists expected — convinced investors that
Federal Reserve rate cuts are off the menu this year, sending Treasury
yields sharply higher. The Nasdaq fell over 4% on Friday alone as a
semiconductor-led slide wiped an estimated $1 trillion off global equity
values, with the S&P 500 down more than 2.5% in that single session.
Good news for the economy was, for one week at least, bad news for
stocks.

European markets fared better but lacked conviction, with the STOXX
Europe 600 easing about half a percent. Investors juggled hopeful
signals — progress in US–Iran negotiations and talk of a possible
Israel–Lebanon ceasefire — against a mid-week announcement from the US
administration of new tariffs of 10–12.5% on a range of countries.

Asia was a mixed bag. Japan's Nikkei 225 eked out a small weekly gain
even as the broader TOPIX slipped, with exporters and tech caught

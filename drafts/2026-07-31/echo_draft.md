# Echo

**25th July to 31st July**

Last Friday, this market couldn't sell its banks fast enough. This
Friday, it couldn't buy its lenders fast enough — one blowout quarter
from **Bajaj Finance** was all it took to change the conversation.

That is the story of a week in which everything that hurt investors a
week ago went into reverse. Crude oil, whose surge toward $100 had
rattled every calculation, retreated nearly 8%. The rupee, freshly
bruised, clawed back more than 1.5% against the dollar. And foreign
investors, sellers for most of the month, returned as buyers as the
week closed.

Between those bookends sat the heaviest earnings week of the quarter —
over a hundred companies a day at its peak — and the market rewarded
the winners generously while punishing the misses without mercy. The
Nifty 50 finished 2.59% higher, unwinding almost all of the previous
week's damage.

## Stocks & Sectors in the News

NBFCs and financial services stole the show. **Bajaj Finance**'s Q1
FY27 numbers ignited a 12%-plus weekly surge to a 52-week high, pulling
**Jio Financial Services** and **Bajaj Finserv** upward in sympathy,
while **IDFC First Bank** and **Federal Bank** made 52-week highs of
their own. The big banks were the quieter half of the story — the Bank
Nifty rose just 1% as investors, burned by the previous week's private
bank results, stayed selective even in a rising market.

Automobiles — including the components, commercial-vehicle and
two-wheeler names — delivered the week's most consistent earnings.
**Mahindra & Mahindra**, **Eicher Motors** and **Maruti Suzuki** were
among the index's best performers on their results days, **TVS Motor
Company** compounded its post-results momentum into an 11% week, and
**Hyundai Motor India** joined the leaders. **Exide Industries**' fresh
52-week high extended the strength into components; commercial-vehicle
sentiment stayed firmer as fuel costs eased with crude's retreat.

Pharma had a quietly excellent week. **Divi's Laboratories** jumped
over 11% to a 52-week high, **Sun Pharmaceutical** reported a 27%
year-on-year rise in Q1 net profit to ₹2,894 crore and touched its own
52-week high, and **Torrent Pharmaceuticals** and **Laurus Labs**
joined the highs list — a sector rewarded for delivering growth in a
market obsessed with earnings.

FMCG split down the middle. **Hindustan Unilever** fell to a 52-week
low as its results failed to convince investors that volume growth is
returning, while **Nestle India**, **Marico** and **United Spirits**
hit 52-week highs — the market is no longer buying "FMCG" as a single
idea; it is paying for specific stories and punishing the rest. **ITC**
lagged on its results day as profit-booking met a cautious outlook.

IT spent the week giving back its rebound. After the previous week's
relief rally, the sector slid more than 2% on Friday alone as investors
booked profits, unwilling to pay up ahead of clearer demand signals —
the sector remains this market's least-trusted recovery.

The PSU and defence pack cooled off. **Bharat Electronics** fell over
4% after Monday's results, **Coal India** slipped about 3%, and the
**Adani** group names — **Adani Ports** and **Adani Energy Solutions**
— eased through their results. After months of strong runs in defence
and infrastructure names, the earnings bar had simply moved higher than
the deliveries.

## Top Delivery-volume Movers (Nifty 500)

**Why do delivery volumes matter?**

Rising delivery volumes for a stock indicate that investors and traders
are taking delivery of the shares of the company. In case this happens
with a rise in prices, then the trend could be durable.

Similarly, falling delivery volumes show that investors and traders
aren't interested in taking delivery of the stock. In case the stock
price rises with a fall in delivery volumes, then the trend might not be
durable.

## FIIs vs DIIs

For most of the month, the foreign-investor story in India has been one
of quiet withdrawal — selling into strength, wary of crude and of a
weakening rupee. This week that story turned decisively: after selling
on Monday, FIIs bought Indian equities on every remaining session, in
growing size each day — over ₹3,600 crore on Thursday alone — ending
the four sessions to Thursday roughly ₹5,700 crore net positive as
cooling oil, a recovering rupee and an earnings season full of winners
gave them names worth paying for.

Domestic institutions played their familiar role of the steady hand —
buyers through the month's turbulence, they spent this week doing what
they have done all year: absorbing whatever the market offered, neither
euphoric in the rally nor shaken in the dips.

**Wondering why FIIs and DIIs generally move in opposite directions?**

*FIIs are more impacted by global events like wars, geopolitical tensions
and trade wars. Their holdings in India are impacted due to foreign
exchange volatility, and any change in monetary policy or fiscal policy
by central banks globally.*

*DIIs on the other hand are mostly concerned by what is happening in
India. When you see FIIs selling shares and DIIs buying them, it's
because sellers will find buyers in the market.*

The energy story finally exhaled. Brent crude fell nearly 8% to around
$89 a barrel as West Asia tensions cooled from a boil to a simmer,
draining the war premium that had driven prices toward $100 — welcome
relief for an oil importer that had spent two weeks watching its import
bill balloon. Natural gas slipped over 3% as comfortable supply kept
buyers unhurried.

Gold ground about 1% higher even as the panic faded — with the US
Federal Reserve holding rates but markets now pricing rate *hikes*
later this year, investors kept some insurance on.

Copper rose nearly 3%, supported by tight supply and steady demand from
power grids and electric-vehicle production, shrugging off the caution
that pinned other risk assets.

## Global Signals

The week's global drama played out in Washington. The US Federal
Reserve held rates steady — but with three regional presidents
dissenting in favour of *higher* rates, the first triple dissent in a
decade — and markets walked away pricing two rate hikes before
year-end. Wall Street stumbled on the news: the Dow fell more than 800
points on decision day, and all three major indices ended the week
lower, with rate-sensitive growth stocks bearing the brunt.
